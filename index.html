01

A statewide gap

About 8.2% of women ages 19–44 were uninsured across California during 2020–2024. This is a population-weighted estimate calculated from county counts, not the average of county percentages.

02

Read extreme rates with care

Sierra has the highest point estimate, 48.6%, but an approximate margin of error of ±32.3 percentage points. That wide interval makes a precise ranking difficult to defend.

03

Local context, not a diagnosis

Los Angeles accounts for the largest uninsured count. Neither a high count nor a high rate tells us how many residents have PCOS, or whether they can obtain appropriate treatment.

The measure

U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2020–2024 five-year estimates, table B27001. Uninsured women in the 19–25, 26–34 and 35–44 age groups are summed and divided by the total female population in those same groups. The universe is the civilian noninstitutionalized population. The source records sex; it does not identify PCOS status or gender identity.

The geography

California's 58 counties, from the official California GIS service credited to the Department of Finance. Boundaries and estimates connect through five-character county FIPS identifiers. County scale provides complete coverage, but conceals neighborhood differences. The boundary service does not establish an exact 2024 vintage. Its older population attributes are not used.

The interpretation

Insurance coverage is one dimension of potential access. These data do not measure specialist availability, affordability, insurance acceptance, actual care received, or unmet need among PCOS patients. They cannot establish causation. Small-county estimates can be unstable, and overlapping intervals should discourage precise rankings. Intervals displayed in charts are clipped to 0–100%.
